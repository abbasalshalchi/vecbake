## 3. The fault being chased

On Apollo–Soyuz, parts that are always in front of the service module
(`apollo_003`) — thrusters `apollo_018.*`, `apollo_002`, `apollo_013`/`014`,
`apollo_024` — are painted over by it in some frames. The worst single pair is
`apollo_002/m` under `apollo_003`.

Painter's algorithm: one depth rank per element per frame. A merged object is
one element. Pairs are compared at the centroid of their *shared* region
(`order._overlap_centre`), which was already the case before this work.

### How it is measured

`audit.run` ray-casts overlapping pixels and checks which element should own
each one. Two sizes were used:

- **Full** — frames 0, 7, 15, 23, 30; 30,000 overlap pixels. About 110 minutes.
- **Screening** — frames 0, 15, 30; 9,000 overlap pixels. About 40 minutes,
  most of it ray-casting. Only compare screening runs with screening runs.

Settings for every run: 1920×1080, merge fills, weld seams, SHARP edges at 30°,
backface cull, see-through, accurate order, chain edges, tol 1 px, stroke 1.6.

## 4. What was tried

### 4.1 Rank constraints by agreement instead of by gap — **worse, reverted**

Idea: a thruster is in front of the body by a hair, but by that hair at every
sample, so ranking a constraint by the size of its depth gap makes it the first
thing the cycle breaker gives up. Score `agree · span + |gap|` instead.

It fixed the synthetic stray-line case in `test_order_cycle.py`. On the real
model (full audit):

| | before | agreement |
|---|---|---|
| errors | 176 | 202 |
| wrong pixels | 1,467 | 1,805 |
| rate | 4.89% | 6.02% |
| order violated | 75 | **102** |

Cause: almost every constraint has high agreement, so the whole graph collapses
into a narrow band of weights just under `span`; the breaker's "weakest tenth"
becomes close to arbitrary. The write-up is in `order.py` beside the gap
weighting, so it is not tried again. (A first version was also 1.8× slower,
from sampling each pair a second time; reusing `_overlap_centre`'s samples
fixed that before the audit.)

### 4.2 Change what the cycle breaker gives up — **ruled out by measurement**

An instrumented bake of Apollo–Soyuz, over 31 ordering passes:

| | |
|---|---|
| sampled pairs | 1,320,490 |
| …with agreement < 0.80 | 16,445 (1.2%) |
| largest stuck component at first break, per pass | 3,714 elements |
| edges inside stuck components | 173,115, of which agreement < 0.80: **474 (0.3%)** |
| constraints given up | 1,496,495, of which agreement ≥ 0.95: **1,478,105 (98.8%)** |

Crossing pairs are rare and under-represented inside cycles. The cycles are
held together by well-supported constraints, so there is no weak class to give
up first. This matches the earlier conclusion in the project notes (four
spending-side attempts, all failed): the lever is finer granularity or better
measurement, not the breaker.

### 4.3 Draw `apollo_003` face by face — **works, but chosen by hand**

Screening audit, forcing one object out of merging with an environment variable:

| | nothing split | `apollo_003` split |
|---|---|---|
| wrong pixels | 446 | **265** |
| rate | 4.96% | **2.94%** |
| errors | 93 | 62 |
| measured wrong | 53 | 26 |
| order violated | 37 | 30 |
| errors involving `apollo_003` | 43 (292 px) | 9 (109 px) |
| elements | 18,324 | 18,338 |
| bake | 748 s | 753 s |

An earlier session recorded that splitting the *next two* objects as well gave
265 → 246 px for 584 more elements, 31% more bake time and a new failure class
("occluder not drawn there" 3 → 7). **Not re-verified.**

That session then built a general rule, `split_span_pct` (default 50%): draw an
object face by face when its depth range averages at least that share of the
scene's, and another object overlapping it on screen sits inside that range.
It is uncommitted, with `scripts/test_deep_body.py` (11 checks, passing). Two
bugs in it were fixed on 13 September — extents were re-appended on every
sampling pass, and an object with no finite depth in a frame shifted all its
later frames against everyone else's.

**The rule is not trustworthy as it stands:**

| depth measured over | Apollo–Soyuz picks | Voyager picks |
|---|---|---|
| every vertex (as built) | `apollo_002`, `apollo_003`, `apollo_024` — the costly case above | `part_001.001` (56.8%, next 33.9%) |
| front-facing vertices only (what `resolve` sees) | `apollo_002`, `apollo_003` — shares 58.2%, 51.7%, 42.6%, 30.6% | not run |

On Apollo–Soyuz there is no clean knee: `apollo_002` passes a 50% bar by 1.7
points. The comment in `bake.py` justifying the default ("Apollo-Soyuz 70.9%
then 32.9%, the Voyager probe 60.7% then 30.2%") **could not be reproduced by
either measure** and should be corrected or removed before anything is
committed.

### 4.4 Read a merged part's depth off its surface — **the cause; too slow as written**

A merged part's depth at a point came from its nearest surface **vertex**
(`pd[d2.argmin()]` in `order._depths_at`). On a low-poly body the vertices are
the corners of long faces, so at the middle of a face the nearest vertex is far
away and the depth read belongs to a corner. Face fills don't have the problem:
a planar face's depth is fitted exactly. That explains why splitting a
low-poly body helped so much for so few elements.

The fix: `bake._surface_tris` hands the ordering pass each merged part's
front-facing triangles; `order._surface_read` interpolates depth inside the
triangle containing the point (inverse depth for perspective; the nearest if
front faces overlap on screen). The nearest sample remains the fallback off the
surface. `scripts/test_surface_depth.py` has 14 checks, passing, and the core
check fails against the old read (the bolt ranks behind the body without
triangles).

Screening audit, splitting disabled:

| | nothing split | `apollo_003` split | **surface read** |
|---|---|---|---|
| wrong pixels | 446 | 265 | **289** |
| rate | 4.96% | 2.94% | **3.21%** |
| measured wrong | 53 | 26 | **31** |
| order violated | 37 | 30 | **30** |
| errors involving `apollo_003` | 43 (292 px) | 9 (109 px) | **24 (170 px)** |
| elements | 18,324 | 18,338 | 18,324 |
| bake | 748 s | 753 s | **1,791 s** |

It removes most of the fault without splitting anything and without a
threshold, which confirms the cause. It does not quite reach splitting
(`apollo_003` keeps one rank per frame, so some cycles remain), and **the bake
is 2.4× slower**. In isolation the ordering pass measured +23% with 50
triangles per merged part and +52% with 800; the rest is per-call overhead
(about fifteen array operations per read against three before, once per
element per overlapping pair).

### 4.5 Solar panels — **not a bug**

`soyuz_029/m` and `soyuz_030/m` bake as four rings, three of them wound as
holes, and on their own paint about 22% of their outline. With everything
drawn, the uncovered share of the wing outline is at most 0.0003 at every
sampled time: each object is a frame, and `soyuz_033` fills its openings. The
earlier "16–20% under-filled" backlog entry, and the "hollow wings" claim on 12
September, were both wrong. Of the 16 multi-ring fills in the bake, the other
14 have no holes at all.
